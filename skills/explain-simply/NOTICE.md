## What was retained from upstream

The explanation craft that makes the original good, largely verbatim in spirit:

- The two-sided failure frame: *too technical* (correct but opaque) versus
  *too simple* (accessible but hollow or wrong), aiming for the middle.
- "Keep the load-bearing technical truth, wrap it in plain words." If a
  simplification makes the idea wrong, back off.
- Pay for your jargon: plain idea first, then attach the real term, so the
  reader owns both and can search it and talk to experts.
- One running example, reused throughout. A second example resets the mental
  model to zero.
- Scale the effort to the difficulty; over-explaining is its own failure.
- Map an analogy part-for-part, and name where it breaks.
